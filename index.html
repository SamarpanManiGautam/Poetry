भूमिका

चित्र कोर्ने चित्रकार र उसको रङहरूसँगको सम्बन्ध जस्तै, मेरो अन्तरमनका द्वन्द्वहरूको साथसाथै मेरो अनगिन्ती आत्मप्रतिरूपणहरूको प्रतिबिम्ब मेरो कवितामा झल्किन्छ। मेरो कवितालाई आकार दिने म मात्र होइन — मेरो जीवनमा मसँग जोडिएका पात्रहरू पनि हुन्। यी पात्रहरूको प्रभाव मेरो लेखाइमा मात्र देखिँदैन, मेरो सामान्य पलहरूलाई समीक्षा गर्ने मेरो मानसिक स्थितिमा पनि उत्तिकै झल्किएको छ।

यस जीवनको किताबमा राखिएका कविताहरूले ती पलहरूलाई चित्रण गर्छन् र ती नाजुक विषयहरूसँग जोडिएका भावनाहरूलाई पोख्छन्।

कसैको भावनालाई केही हरफका कविताले पूर्ण रूपमा व्यक्त गर्न सम्भव छैन, तर त्यो भावनाको बोझ केही हल्का पार्न यी कविताहरू एक भारी बिसाउने चौतारी बन्दछन्।

०१

उदासीनता

समर्पण मणि गौतम

छाएको कालो रंग,
हराउँदै छु म, समाउँदैछु म यहीँ।
लाग्छ, साथी मेरो कालो रात मात्र,
बिहानीको उजेलीले ल्याउँछ उदासीझैँ लाग्छ।

म हिड्छु, म हिड्छु, छैन मेरो कुनै गन्तव्य,
हजारौँ भेट्छु, हजारौँ देख्छु, तर अन्तिममा म एक्लो नै पाउँछु।
एकान्त खोज्छु, कसैको न्यानो अंगालो खोज्छु,
कसैलाई भन्न खोज्छु, तर बाँधिएको महसुस गर्छु।

अन्धकारको पर्खाइमा हुन्छु म, उज्यालो देख्दा डराउँछु म,
जिउँदो लाशझैँ छु म, र संसार चाहिँ मेरो चिहानझैँ छ।
मेरो शरीर थाकेको छ, अनियन्त्रित निन्द्राको वशमा मेरो शरीर छ,
म आज आफैँसँग हारेको छु, र आफ्नै मरणको पर्खाइमा छु।

म हारेको छु।

०२

आलोचना

समर्पण मणि गौतम

तिम्रा ती सेता वस्त्रले तिम्रो सिन्दूरझैँ रातो रङ्गले रङ्गिएका पाप ढाक्दैन।
न त तिम्रो धर्मको मुखौटाले तिम्रो कुरूप व्यक्तित्व नै!
तिम्रो अस्तित्व नै कसैको शोक मनाउनुको कारण छ भने, तिम्रो मरण अर्थहीन छ।

हो, म पापी हुँ, म एक तुच्छ प्राणी हुँ।
मलाई मेरो कर्मको अफसोस छैन, र तिमीलाई पनि म निस्खोट देख्दिन।
कहाँ थियौ तिमी, जब मेरो अन्तसकरणदेखि म मदतका लागि रोइरहेको थिएँ?
कहाँ थियौ तिमी, जब मेरो आवाज बन्द कोठामा प्रतिध्वनि भई गुन्जिरहे,
र मेरो रुवाइ शून्यतामा परिणत भइ गए?

कहाँ थियौ तिमी।

०३

देह त्याग

समर्पण मणि गौतम

तिम्रो याद मेटाउन आज आफ्नो नैतिकतालाई दागबत्ती दिएँ ।
तिम्रो हात छुटाउन आफ्नो देहमा बास छोडिदिएँ ।

मैले जितेको लाडाई आज मेरो हार मानिदिएँ ।
मेरो कर्मलाई तिमी प्रतिको घात सोचिदिएँ ।

मेरो हात र पाउ तिमीसंग हिँडेको बाटो देख्दा दिशा मोडिदिन्छ ।
मेरो बोली कसैलाई तीर झैँ लाग्ला भनी तरकसमा बसिदिन्छ ।

त्यही पनि मेरो मौनताले धेरै शब्द बोलिदिन्छ र मेरो मृत शरीरले भोलिको आशा मारिदिन्छ ।

०४

लाञ्छना

समर्पण मणि गौतम

आज मेरो अँगालोमा छौ तर तिम्रो मन र मस्तिष्क केही अरूसँग गासेको छौ।
यो यात्रा कुनै गन्तव्यबिनाको छ र तिमी र मेरो साथ बिनाअर्थको छ।

के, किन र कसरीको उत्तर मसँग छैन र यस सम्बन्धको अस्तित्व संसारको लागि अनैतिक छ।
हाम्रो कर्मको यातनाले गर्दा कसैको शुद्ध प्रेममाथिको विश्वासको हत्या भएको छ।

हामी दोषी ठहरिएका छौँ र हाम्रो साथलाई विश्वासघातको छाप लगाइएको छ।
आज दोषी, पीडित र प्रहरी एकै जालमा जेलिएको छ र हाम्रो गल्तीको सजाय कोही अरूनै भोग्दै छ।

०५

चिहान

समर्पण मणि गौतम

मेरो बास चिहानमा छ।
आत्मा मेरो साथमा छ।
शरीर मेरो हजारौं छ।
तर लास मेरो गलेको छ।

रात मेरो कालो छ।
र जुन रगतले रंगिएको छ।
अनेक आवाज गुन्जिँदा पनि
संसार मेरो शून्य छ।

मेरो शरीर एक्लो छ।
बन्द बाकसमा राखिएको छ।
ढकढकाउँदा कोहीले खोलिदेला भन्ने मेरो आशा छ।
निसास्सिएर, गुम्सिएर बसेका भावना पोख्न आतुर छ।

मेरा अपूर्ण कार्य पूरा गर्न
मेरा रहर र इच्छा पूरा गर्न अब मेरो कंकाल बाँकी छ।
यो संसारमा म अदृश्य छु।
तर मेरो अस्तित्व अनिष्ठ छ।

मलाई छुडाइदेऊ यो बन्धनदेखि।
यो जालदेखि।
यो अन्धकारदेखि।
यो संसारदेखि।

०६

अन्तर्द्वन्द्व

समर्पण मणि गौतम

मृत्युको निम्तो, र जीवनको अवकाश।
अनन्तको सूर्य ग्रहण, त्रासको आभास।

अर्ध जीवित भावना, केवल निराशाको निवास।
निश्चैताको अभाव, देखिने भविष्य राख।

विश्वासको हत्या, अन्यायको सुरुवात।

मरुभूमीको चीत्कार, वर्षाको पर्खाइ।
नर्कको भूमिमा, स्वर्गको लडाई।

Reflection

उदासीनता

Udasinta · Melancholy / Apathy

Black spreads everywhere. The speaker is dissolving “right here.” The only companion is the night, and even dawn feels like sadness. He walks with no destination, meets thousands, sees thousands, and still ends up alone. He seeks solitude but also someone’s warm embrace, and wants to tell someone but feels bound. He is afraid of light, a “living corpse,” and the world has become his chihan, his mere trace. His body is tired, ruled by uncontrolled sleep. He has been defeated by himself, and he lives in the shadow of his own death.

The philosophyThis is the heart of the collection. Notice the contradiction: he seeks solitude and an embrace, and fears light and longs for it. Depression is rarely a clean wish; it is two opposing wishes frozen together. The most chilling line is that the world has become his imprint: he is no longer a person in the world, but a mark left behind by a person.

Reflection

आलोचना

Alochana · Criticism

Here the speaker turns on the beloved. Your white clothes can’t hide sins dyed red like your vermilion, and your mask of religion can’t hide your ugly self. (Notice the image of sindoor, the red mark of marriage, set against white: purity and sin in one body.) Then comes a brutal honesty: Yes, I am a sinner. I feel no remorse. And I don’t see you as innocent either.

Then the question that breaks the poem open: Where were you when I was crying for help from my conscience? Where were you when my voice echoed in a closed room and my cries turned into emptiness?

The philosophyThis is moral injury as an accusation. The speaker refuses to be the only guilty one. The real wound isn’t the sin but the absence, the silence when someone needed a witness. The repeated “Where were you?” is the oldest question humans ask of the universe too.

Reflection

देह त्याग

Dehatyag · Renouncing the Body

The title echoes the Hindu-Buddhist idea of leaving the body. Here it reads as a gift wrapped in surrender: to erase your memory, I set my morality on fire; to free your hands, I leave my fragrance in my body; the battles I won, I now concede as lost; I thought my karma was an attack on you. His hands and feet change direction when they see the path he once walked with her. He keeps his tongue ambushed in silence, afraid his words might pierce someone like an arrow. The last line is the most haunting: his silence speaks many words, and his dead body kills tomorrow’s hope.

The philosophyThis is self-erasure as misguided love. He believes disappearing will free the other person. But the final line quietly undoes that logic: even in imagining his absence, he realizes it would destroy someone’s hope. The poem argues with itself. Silence, which was meant to protect, turns out to be a language of its own.

Reflection

लाञ्छना

Lanchhana · Stigma / Blame

The speaker stands in the beloved’s courtyard, but the beloved’s mind is tangled up with someone else. The journey has no destination, and their togetherness is “meaningless.” The relationship is anaitik (immoral) in the world’s eyes, and the pain of their karma has murdered someone else’s pure trust. Then comes the poem’s cruelest insight: the guilty, the victim, and the perpetrator are caught in the same net, and the punishment for their mistake is paid by others.

The philosophyThis is the tragedy of desire colliding with morality. No one is purely villain or victim. Love becomes a debt that innocent third parties repay. Sin spreads like ripples, and you never get to choose who drowns.

Reflection

चिहान

Chihan · Mark / Trace

This is a poem about being a ghost while alive. His scent lingers in the trace, his soul is with him, but his body is “in the thousands,” scattered, while his corpse has already decayed. The night is black, the moon is dyed with blood, many voices echo, yet his world is empty. His body is lonely, kept in a sealed box, and he hopes someone will open it while his heart still beats. Unspoken feelings sit choking, desperate to burst out. Only his skeleton remains to complete his unfinished work. He is invisible in the world, yet his existence is anishta (ill-omened). It ends with a plea: free me from this bond, this web, this darkness, this world.

The philosophyChihan means both a “sign” and what remains of someone. Here lies the idea that a person can be dead long before the body stops. The “sealed box” is the self, and the tragic detail is the hope that “someone will open it” while the heart still beats. Even in despair there is a thin thread reaching toward another person. The plea at the end speaks to a release that is as much about being seen as about leaving.

Reflection

अन्तर्द्वन्द्व

Antardwanda · Inner Conflict

This poem is a list of paradoxes: an invitation to death and a holiday from life; a solar eclipse of the eternal sun; half-alive feelings, a home made only of hopelessness; the future visible only as ash; the murder of trust and the beginning of injustice; the scream of a desert and the waiting for rain. It ends in “hell’s land, heaven’s war,” followed by a stray, unfinished “and...”

The philosophyEvery line is a contradiction because the mind itself is at war. Nothing is resolved; it is simply allowed to stand. The final dangling “and” is perhaps the most honest part: the suffering doesn’t conclude, it just trails off. It echoes Camus: the absurd isn’t solved, it is lived.
